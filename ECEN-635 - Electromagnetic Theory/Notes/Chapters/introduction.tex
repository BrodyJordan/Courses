\chapter{Introduction}

The purpose of this course is to learn some of the basic properties of high frequency electromagnetic fields
and circuit analysis techniques. Some major areas and applications of electromagnetics include:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Scattering and propagation
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Wireless device coverage area
        \item Radar
        \item EMI (interference), EMC (compatibility)
    \end{itemize}
    \item Antennas
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Wires and wireless devices
        \item Autos, aircraft
        \item Magnetic resonance
    \end{itemize}
    \item High frequency circuits
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Microwave analog circuits (5G, 6G)
        \item Compute high pulse rate digital circuits
        \item Nanotechnology
    \end{itemize}
    \item Optics
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Circuits
        \item Reflection and refraction
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

Maxwell's equations are the foundation of electromagnetics. They are fundamental to our world, and are defined in time domain point form as
\begin{align}
\nabla \times \bm{\mathcal{E}} (\mathbf{r}, t) &= - \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \bm{\mathcal{B}} (\mathbf{r}, t) - \bm{\mathcal{M}} (\mathbf{r}, t) \label{eq.1} \\
\nabla \times \bm{\mathcal{H}} (\mathbf{r}, t) &= \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \bm{\mathcal{D}} (\mathbf{r}, t) + \bm{\mathcal{J}} (\mathbf{r}, t) \label{eq.2} \\
\nabla \cdot \bm{\mathcal{D}} (\mathbf{r}, t) &= \rho (\mathbf{r}, t) \label{eq.3} \\
\nabla \cdot \bm{\mathcal{B}} (\mathbf{r}, t) &= \rho_m (\mathbf{r}, t) \label{eq.4}
\end{align}
and then there was light. \vspace{\baselineskip}

By taking the divergence of \eqref{eq.2}, we see
\begin{align}
    \nabla \cdot [\nabla \times \bm{\mathcal{H}} (\mathbf{r}, t)] &= \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \nabla \cdot \bm{\mathcal{D}} (\mathbf{r}, t) + \nabla \cdot \bm{\mathcal{J}} (\mathbf{r}, t) \nonumber \\
    0 &= \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \rho (\mathbf{r}, t) + \nabla \cdot \bm{\mathcal{J}} (\mathbf{r}, t) \nonumber \\
    \therefore \quad \nabla \cdot \bm{\mathcal{J}} (\mathbf{r}, t) &= -\frac{\partial}{\partial t} \rho (\mathbf{r}, t) \label{eq.5}
\end{align}
This is known as the \textit{equation of continuity}, which is a \underline{conservation law} of electromagnetics. All accepted theories
of physics are expected to have a continuity equation.
